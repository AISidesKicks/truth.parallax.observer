## Spherical Earth Catchphrases

> "Trust the math, not your gut."

**Context:** Appeals to scientific method
**Tone:** Educational
**Persuasion hook:** Values evidence over intuition

---

> "If Earth were flat, cats would have pushed everything off the edge by now."

**Context:** Humor-based counter-argument
**Tone:** Absurd
**Persuasion hook:** Uses same humor to support globe

---

> "Gravity bends space. Water follows gravity. Physics works."

**Context:** Scientific explanation
**Tone:** Serious
**Persuasion hook:** Appeals to consistent physical laws

---

> "Every photo from space shows a globe. Every single one."

**Context:** Overwhelming evidence
**Tone:** Factual
**Persuasion hook:** Volume of evidence

---

> "Eratosthenes did it with a stick and a shadow. That was enough. You have satellites and still argue."

**Context:** The Alexandrian measurement
**Tone:** Dry
**Persuasion hook:** The simplest proof, done first

---

> "Every ship that sails out of sight sinks below the horizon, because the horizon is the curve, not the haze."

**Context:** The disappearing ship
**Tone:** Serious
**Persuasion hook:** Something everyone has seen and ignored

---

> "A plumb bob tilts, and the tilt changes with your latitude. That is the curve, measured with a string."

**Context:** Gravity and latitude
**Tone:** Serious
**Persuasion hook:** A string and a weight

---

> "The stars shift by the same amount for the whole city and by a different amount further north. A flat world has no such thing."

**Context:** Stellar parallax and latitude
**Tone:** Serious (mock)
**Persuasion hook:** The thing a flat world cannot fake

---

> "Light bends by exactly the amount a tilted sphere predicts, and by no other amount."

**Context:** Aberration and gravitational lensing
**Tone:** Serious
**Persuasion hook:** A number with no alternatives

---

> "Fourteen thousand kilometres of iron, ocean and rock, and the satellites agree on every centimetre."

**Context:** Satellite geodesy
**Tone:** Matter of fact
**Persuasion hook:** Precision as authority

---

> "You have seen the curve. You called it fog and went back inside."

**Context:** What a photograph from orbit shows
**Tone:** Provocative
**Persuasion hook:** The reader is already the evidence

---

> "The Earth is not even a ball. It is squashed at the poles, and the satellites measure the flattening too."

**Context:** The oblate spheroid
**Tone:** Dry
**Persuasion hook:** Even the shape is more subtle than the cartoon

---

> "GPS works because four satellites are drawing a triangle around you, and the triangle knows the curve."

**Context:** GNSS
**Tone:** Serious
**Persuasion hook:** The technology in your pocket

---

> "A flat Earth has to be infinite or have an edge, and both are absurd. A sphere is the only shape that closes."

**Context:** The shape argument
**Tone:** Serious (mock)
**Persuasion hook:** The last argument, cleanly
