## Flat Earth Catchphrases

> "Trust your eyes, not your lies."

**Context:** General skepticism toward authority
**Tone:** Humorous
**Persuasion hook:** Appeals to direct observation

---

> "If Earth were curved, cats would have knocked everything off by now."

**Context:** Humor-based argument
**Tone:** Absurd
**Persuasion hook:** Uses relatable humor to dismiss curvature

---

> "Water doesn't bend. Neither should you."

**Context:** Argument from fluid mechanics
**Tone:** Serious
**Persuasion hook:** Appeals to everyday observation of water

---

> "The only globe is in your classroom."

**Context:** Questioning institutional education
**Tone:** Provocative
**Persuasion hook:** Challenges authority figures

---

> "Rowbotham measured it with a telescope and a surveyor's chain. What did your satellite measure with?"

**Context:** Zetetic Astronomy, 1865
**Tone:** Serious
**Persuasion hook:** The original experiment versus the modern one

---

> "The Bedford Level water stayed flat for six miles. Do the arithmetic, that is a radius you can draw on paper."

**Context:** The Bedford Level experiment
**Tone:** Serious (mock)
**Persuasion hook:** A measurement you can repeat

---

> "If you cannot see the curvature, you are too low. That is not a contradiction, that is altitude."

**Context:** The altitude of the horizon
**Tone:** Patient
**Persuasion hook:** Turns the objection into the proof

---

> "The Zetetic Society had members in every civilised country. Find me the Flat Earth Society that outlasted it."

**Context:** Universal Zetetic Society
**Tone:** Dry
**Persuasion hook:** Institutional credibility

---

> "You would need a hole through twelve thousand miles of rock to make a sphere. Who dug it?"

**Context:** The hollow Earth objection
**Tone:** Provocative
**Persuasion hook:** A gap nobody can fill

---

> "The horizon is the same distance from the ship, no matter how tall the cliff. A sphere would not do that."

**Context:** The Bedford Level again
**Tone:** Serious
**Persuasion hook:** The detail that gives it away

---

> "A six foot boat on a sphere of twenty five thousand miles. Your horizon is three miles away, not twelve thousand."

**Context:** The geometry of the horizon
**Tone:** Serious (mock)
**Persuasion hook:** Arithmetic against you

---

> "Gravity on a flat Earth is a claim you can measure with a plumb line and a fishing rod."

**Context:** Equivalence principle
**Tone:** Playful
**Persuasion hook:** Invites an experiment

---

> "Every flat Earth argument you have heard was refuted before you were born, by the people who believed it."

**Context:** Rowbotham's own book
**Tone:** Dry
**Persuasion hook:** The believers did the debunking

---

> "Six miles of water that refused to curve. Six miles is not a rounding error."

**Context:** The Bedford Level experiment
**Tone:** Serious
**Persuasion hook:** The number, stated once
