## Norse Quotes

**Source:** Poetic Edda, Prose Edda (Snorri Sturluson), and modern scholars of Norse cosmology
**URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norse_cosmology

### The world tree

> "An ash I know, Yggdrasil, whose waters are white, the great tree's limbs hide. There are three roots to it, and three branches spread over the world."

> "The tree's trunk is the world tree, and its branches reach into the heavens, while its roots delve into the domains of giants and the dead."

### The nine worlds

> "Nine worlds were those, and I am ninth among them, the great Yggdrasil, and I stand upon the sea, and I hold the bridge of heaven."

> "There are nine realms in the Norse cosmos, and Midgard, where mankind dwells, is the one that sits in the middle of them all."

### Fate and the runes

> "I hung on the windy tree, hung there for nights full of nine, wounded by a spear, sacrificed to Odin, myself to myself, on the tree that none may ever know what roots beneath it run."

> "The Norns spin the threads of fate at the foot of Yggdrasil, the tree of the world."

### The way between the worlds

> "The rainbow bridge, Bifrost, connects Asgard, the realm of the gods, to Midgard, the world of humans."

### The creator and the void

> "In the beginning there was nothing, which exploded."

> "The land took shape as a plane, with the sea around it and the sky above it, and the void below it."

### Death and the other side

> "The dead travel down to Hel, where the ruler of the same name holds dominion over the underworld, and the walls of Hel are of rain and the doors are of stone."

### The fire and the end

> "A fire will be lit in the south, and the sun will vanish, and the stars will fall from the sky, and the mountains will collapse, and the world will be submerged in water."

### From the Eddas, in the Bellows translation

**Source:** The Poetic Edda, translated by Henry Adams Bellows, 1923
**URL:** https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Poetic_Edda_(tr._Bellows)

> "An ash I know, Yggdrasil its name, with water white is the great tree wet; thence come the dews that fall in the dales, green by Urth's well does it ever grow."

> "Thence come the maidens mighty in wisdom, three from the dwelling down 'neath the tree; Urth is one named, Verthandi the next, and Skuld the third. Laws they made there, and life allotted to the sons of men, and set their fates."

> "Three roots there are that three ways run 'neath the ash-tree Yggdrasil; 'neath the first lives Hel, 'neath the second the frost-giants, 'neath the last are the lands of men."

> "Ratatosk is the squirrel who there shall run on the ash-tree Yggdrasil; from above the words of the eagle he bears, and tells them to Nithhogg beneath."

> "For heaven's bridge burns all in flame, and the sacred waters seethe."

> "There hangs a wolf by the western door, and o'er it an eagle hovers."

> "I ween that I hung on the windy tree, hung there for nights full nine; with the spear I was wounded, and offered I was to Othin, myself to myself, on the tree that none may ever know what root beneath it runs."

> "Yggdrasil shakes, and shiver on high the ancient limbs, and the giant is loose."

> "How fare the gods? how fare the elves? All Jotunheim groans, the gods are at council."

### More from the Eddas

> "I saw, and behold a tree in the midst of the earth, and the height of it was three nights."

> "Did not the Baldr god, when he was born, all the gods marvel? For from his face the light of all the world came out."

> "Now I see the earth anew, all green again from the waves, the cataracts fall, and the eagle flies, and fish he catches beneath the cliffs."

> "The four harts run through the wood, the four harts that are watched by the gods."

> "Where the serpent's tail is, the sun is not, and the sun's word follows the moon's word."

### Modern reading

> "Norse cosmology, with its vast network of gods, giants, and mystical realms, continues to captivate those who seek wisdom in the ancient world."

> "The Norse worldview was not a single system but a patchwork, and different sources disagree about the shape and the number of the worlds."
